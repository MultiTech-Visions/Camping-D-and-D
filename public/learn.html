How to Play

Everything you need to know, in the time it takes a marshmallow to toast.

Characters My Sheet How to Play

1 · What this is

Campfire Saga is collaborative storytelling. The GM spins the tale; you say what your hero tries to do; a handful of physical dice decide how it goes. There's no math homework, no rulebooks to memorize, and the app never rolls for you — the dice live in a tray that gets passed around the fire.

2 · Making your character

Pick a name and a one-line concept (“runaway storm-priest with a debt to pay”). Then spend 4 points across four attributes — max 2 in any one at creation. Constitution may even be 0 if you want to be a glass cannon. Add an optional flavor label (pure cosmetics — your magic might be “lightning” or “whiskey-fueled luck”) and a hidden desire that only the GM ever sees.

As you raise an attribute in the builder, you'll see its green dice appear — that's not decoration, that's literally your dice pool.

3 · The four attributes

  • Brawn — muscle, force, smashing and shoving.
  • Constitution — toughness, and your buffer: it soaks failures so your other attributes don't fray.
  • Magic — the strange and the supernatural, whatever your flavor says it looks like.
  • Wits — cleverness, speed of thought, noticing the thing everyone missed.

The key rule: an attribute's rank is exactly how many dice you roll with it.

4 · Rank → dice

Nobody starts with a yellow die. Yellows — and their mighty Triumph face — are earned by raising an attribute to rank 4 or 5 through play.

5 · How a turn works

Go around the circle. On your turn, describe what you do. The GM names the difficulty by dropping negative dice (purple, maybe red or black) into the tray; you add your attribute's dice (plus any blue boosts). Roll once. Read it together. The GM almost never rolls — monsters act through the consequences of your roll: succeed with threat and the troll still clips you; fail outright and it gets its whole turn for free.

6 · Reading the dice — THE cheat sheet

  • Success vs Failure cancel 1-for-1. At least one success left over = it works. Ties fail.
  • Advantage vs Threat cancel 1-for-1, separately from success/failure. You can succeed with threat, or fail with advantage.
  • Triumph = a success plus something great. Despair = a failure plus something terrible. They never cancel each other.

8 · Spending advantage

Leftover advantage is yours to spend: make your success flashier, fill an extra clock segment (every 2 advantage), or hand an ally a blue die — “I shove the lantern in the guard's eyes; Mira, take a boost for your sneak past.” A single leftover advantage is a small flourish or one blue die.

9 · Getting hurt

When you fail, the attribute you used drains one rank for the rest of the encounter — and since rank = dice, your best dice (the yellows!) fray first. Or you can spend 1 Constitution instead to protect your specialty. An attribute at 0 can't be used at all. Everything refills when the encounter ends. But if your Constitution is at 0 and you fail again… that's when the GM decides what “down for the count” means.

10 · Winning & danger: clocks

Forget hit points. Every obstacle is a progress clock — fill its segments with successes and you win. Meanwhile the GM may run a danger clock that fills on your threats and despairs (about 1 segment per 2 threats — a plain miss does not advance it). Most fights are a race between the two. Some clocks are secret until the story reveals them…

11 · Getting stronger

Every few encounters (the GM sets the pace, usually 3) you gain +1 attribute point, up to rank 5. That's the whole progression — and it's how you earn those yellow dice. No feats, no levels, no homework. Now grab a marshmallow; you already know how to play.
